# Illustration 03: one long scroll versus a tray of index cards, a person deciding

## Composition
A hand-drawn two-sided scene on cream paper with a person standing in the
narrow gap between them, no words.

- LEFT SIDE (bulkier, occupying about 45% of the width): a single very long
  paper scroll standing upright, coiled at its base, unrolling all the way to the
  top edge of the frame. Its whole surface is dense with tightly packed
  horizontal ink lines, one continuous column, so reading it visibly requires
  going through the entire length. A small hand-drawn arrow curls from a knot at
  the bottom of the scroll all the way up to its top, meaning "the whole thing
  has to come out".
- RIGHT SIDE: a shallow wooden card tray holding a fan of separate index cards
  standing on edge, roughly thirty of them, each with just one short dash near
  the top. Three or four cards have been pulled out of the tray and are floating
  slightly to the left, tilted, as if being picked individually without touching
  the rest. The tray has a small tab sticking out of its front edge.
- CENTER: a simply drawn person, full body, small, standing between the two,
  turned three-quarters toward the tray, one hand reaching toward the floating
  cards and the other hand still resting on the scroll's coil. No facial detail.
  The person is undecided, not triumphant.
- FLOOR: one thin ground line, a couple of stray pencil ticks, one small crumpled
  paper ball near the tray leg.

The two objects must read as the same kind of content at two different loading
granularities: one indivisible roll versus many individually liftable cards.
