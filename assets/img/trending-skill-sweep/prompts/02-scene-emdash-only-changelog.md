# Illustration 02: one long scroll is full of dashes, every handwritten page is clean

## Composition
A hand-drawn still life on cream paper, two unequal halves, no words.

- LEFT HALF (about 40% of the width): one long vertical scroll that unrolls off
  the top of the frame. Its surface is crowded with short horizontal dashes,
  drawn in dense rows — the dashes are the whole point, and they are clearly the
  same mark repeated dozens of times at slightly different lengths and angles so
  the pattern looks machine-stacked. The scroll has a wooden dowel at its bottom
  end and a faint "printed by machine" feel: perfectly even row spacing, and a
  small gear-and-roller mechanism sketched at its base, feeding it out.
- RIGHT HALF: four or five separate handwritten sheets pinned or leaning
  against each other, each with only two or three sparse ink lines and generous
  empty space. One sheet is a folded booklet. A pen lies across the lowest
  sheet. These must look unmistakably hand-made: uneven baselines, wobbly
  strokes, a smudge.
- BETWEEN THE TWO HALVES: a thin dashed vertical divider, and one single long
  dash drawn crossing that divider from the scroll side, as if it leaked through.
- BOTTOM RIGHT: a small magnifying glass hovering over one handwritten sheet,
  and under the glass the paper is empty.

The contrast the image must carry: crowding on the automatically produced
object, cleanliness on the human-written ones.
