# Cover: the agent that could not finish its own picture

## Composition
One wide hand-drawn scene, 16:9, no words anywhere in the image.

A small, friendly robot sits at a drafting table and is drawing a system
architecture diagram onto a big sheet of cream paper. The robot holds a pen in
one mechanical hand and is mid-stroke. The diagram on the paper is a neat
network of rounded boxes connected by arrows — but the ink line the robot is
currently drawing runs off the edge of the paper and simply stops in empty air.

Where the line stops, three obstacles sit on the same desk, drawn as ordinary
objects rather than icons:
- a chunky hard drive with a cracked lid and a small pool of spilled dust
  beside it, meaning "out of disk space"
- a network cable yanked half out of a wall socket, the plug dangling
- a stack of heavy bricks labelled only by shape (no text) pressing down on a
  small browser window, meaning "the toolchain is 374 MB"

The robot looks at the dangling cable, not at the viewer. Nothing is broken
dramatically; this is a mild, wry scene, not a disaster scene.

## ZONES
- CENTER-LEFT (main subject): robot at the drafting table, the half-finished
  architecture drawing, ink line running off the paper edge
- RIGHT: the three obstacles (cracked drive, dangling cable, bricks on a
  browser window), stacked with generous gaps
- BACKGROUND: warm cream paper texture, faint grid, one soft coffee ring stain
  in a corner, plenty of empty space around the header area

## COLORS
- Cream paper background (#FDF6E3)
- Near-black ink (#1F2429) for all linework, slightly imperfect hand-drawn
  strokes with visible wobble
- Soft coral (#FF6B6B) for the stopped ink line and the dangling plug
- Mint green (#88D8B0) for the completed part of the diagram
- Warm yellow (#FFD93D) for the lamp glow over the drafting table
- Muted grey-blue (#9AA5B1) for the drive and bricks

## STYLE
- Hand-drawn sketch-notes aesthetic, like a careful engineer's notebook page
- Flat, no gradients, no 3D, no photorealism, no glossy plastic
- Line weight varies by hand; hatching for shadows
- 45-55% of the canvas stays as breathing room, mostly upper third
