A small wooden table loom sits slightly left of center, seen at a three-quarter angle. Instead of
thread, its warp carries a grid of tiny square tiles — each tile a single hand-inked cell, some
filled with a dot, some with a short diagonal stroke, some with a tiny curve, so the woven cloth
emerging from the beader reads as a coarse character-mosaic landscape: a low hill, a sliver of
moon, a few strokes of water. The mosaic is clearly built one cell at a time, and only a handful
of cells near the falling edge are still loose, mid-flight, half threaded onto the cloth.

The shuttle rests on the cloth, and one thin spare ribbon of mosaic trails off the loom toward the
viewer — a narrow strip only a few tiles wide, clean and economical, the only strip actually used.

To the right, on the floor beside the loom, sits a tall untidy stack of identical small cloth
offcuts, hundreds of them, all the same size, most still on the same little squares of paper. They
were cut but never taken. They should visually dominate the vertical space on the right side while
staying low-contrast and slightly dusty, so the eye goes to the loom first and to the pile second.

dusty slate for the mosaic tiles, one pale amber highlight on the shuttle. Loose black ink
outlines with visible wobble, flat hand-drawn fills, no gradients, no photorealism, no gloss.
