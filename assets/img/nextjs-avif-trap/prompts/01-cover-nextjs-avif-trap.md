# Cover: a switch that got thrown off, then back on — and the wire leads somewhere else

## Composition
Single wide hand-drawn vignette on cream paper, 16:9. Two states of the SAME
wall toggle switch sit left and right, connected by a long dashed arc. The
cable from the switch does NOT run to the stack of boxes behind it; it drops
down through the paper into a small round valve that a magnifying glass is
hovering over. 55–60% of the canvas stays empty cream.

## ZONES
- LEFT ZONE (lower-left third): the toggle switch thrown DOWN. Its bulb is
  drawn hollow and grey, with a single coral slash across it. Loose ink hatching
  under the switch plate to read as "off".
- RIGHT ZONE (right third, slightly higher): the same toggle switch thrown UP.
  Its bulb is filled warm yellow with short radiating stroke lines. A mint-green
  double underline beneath the plate.
- CENTER: a hand-drawn dashed arc from the left switch to the right switch.
  Exactly six small tally strokes sit along the arc, evenly spaced, no numerals.
- LOWER CENTER: the power cable leaves the right switch, bends down and
  disappears through a torn hole in the paper. Below the tear, in "cutaway"
  style, the cable reconnects to a small round valve with a pointer dial.
  A simple magnifying glass circle overlaps the valve.
- BACKGROUND: a stack of three plain wooden boxes (no labels, no marks on their
  faces) drawn in faint grey ink at upper-left, behind everything. The dashed
  line that would connect those boxes to the switch is drawn broken and crossed
  once, so the reader sees that this connection is NOT the real one.
