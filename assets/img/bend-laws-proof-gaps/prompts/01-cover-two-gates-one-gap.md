# Cover prompt — two gates, one gap

A wide, quiet flat-vector illustration. No words, no letters, no numbers, no
logos, no watermark, no signature anywhere in the image.

Scene: an interior cross-section of a low stone corridor seen straight from the
side, running left to right. The corridor is divided into two halves by a single
heavy lintel gate in the middle.

LEFT HALF (the silent way): a shallow ramp. Small flat rectangular tiles —
simplified, uniform, matte — slide from the left edge and pass *under* the gate
through a narrow unguarded gap beneath the lintel, one of them already through
and tipping slightly out of line with the others. Nothing stops them. A faint
dashed arrow marks their path. The tile that slipped through is drawn one
notch out of register with its neighbours — visible, but not dramatized.

RIGHT HALF (the proof way): the same tiles must pass *through* the gate opening
itself. The front-most tile is stopped dead against the stone, pressed flat
against a carved bracket-shaped relief on the lintel (an abstract shape only —
no readable characters). A short, restrained burst of thin radial lines at the
contact point. Behind it the queue of tiles is neatly aligned and intact.

The gate itself: warm pale stone with a subtle paper-grain texture, drawn with
clean geometric edges, one engraved horizontal band across the lintel.

Palette: elegant and cool — slate blue-grey background wash occupying most of
the frame, off-white paper tone for the corridor floor, muted graphite for the
tiles, and a single amber accent used only at the two contact points (the
tipping tile on the left, the blocked tile on the right). No other saturated
colour.

Composition: visual anchor sits slightly right of centre; 45–55% empty
breathing room, mostly in the upper-left sky area; strong horizontal baseline;
no vignette, no drop shadows heavier than 4% opacity.

Mood: calm, technical, dry — a diagram that was drawn carefully, not a poster
that is trying to excite. Absolutely no text of any kind.
